## Gameplay (agreed, and built in `street-core.js`)

Road Rash-style racing and fighting, tuned in `apps/street-kids/street-core.js`, which has 20 passing unit tests (`test/logic.test.mjs`). Keep these rules unless the owner changes them; only the drawing is being replaced.

- **A race:** you and six rivals (Kalu, Bablu, Mintu, Rocky, Tinku, Pinky) on a city road with bends and hills, you starting last on the grid after a 3, 2, 1, Go! countdown. The first race is 2.4 km, and each one after is 300 m longer, up to 4 km. The bike speeds up on its own to 216 km/h; you steer, brake and fight. The kerb slows you to 79 km/h, and its lamp posts, palms and trees throw you off.
- **Traffic:** rickshaws, CNG auto-rickshaws, painted trucks, buses and cars drive along in lanes. Riding into the back of one is a crash.
- **Fighting:** punch (quick, short reach), kick (longer reach, harder, shoves the rival across the road), and weapons. Some rivals carry a **stick, chain or nunchaku**; knock them off their bike and you take it, and from then on a punch swings it. A crash drops it. Rivals fight back. A rival in reach shows their name and health.
- **Health:** rivals' blows and crashes (20 each) take it away; at zero you're knocked out and the game is over.
- **Scoring:** 100 points for each rival knocked off; 1000, 600 and 300 for finishing first, second and third, times the race's number. Finish in the top three to go on to the next race, with full health; finish fourth or lower and the game ends.
- **Controls:**
  - Keyboard: ← → (or A / D) steer, ↓ (or S) brake, X (or J) punch, Z (or K) kick, P / Esc pause.
  - Touch: drag sideways to steer, tap to punch, flick up to kick, flick down to brake, tap the score to pause.
- **Saving:** a race in progress survives a reload and comes back paused (`bdnix_streetkids_save`); a race just won saves as the start of the next. The best score is kept in `bdnix_streetkids_best`.
- **Credit line:** "Inspired by Road Rash, created by Electronic Arts in 1991. Road Rash is a trademark of its owner; bdnix isn't affiliated with or endorsed by them." "Road Rash" appears nowhere else: `tests/ui/checks.mjs` and `tools/boundaries.mjs` now check that.

## Graphics: what was tried and rejected

Don't repeat these.

1. **2D canvas, flat vector shapes** (`apps/street-kids/street.js`, still on the branch): a synthwave dusk city, a pseudo-3D road and cartoon riders. Rejected as "childlike" and "too unrealistic". It is to be **replaced**.
2. **three.js scene with bikes, riders and vehicles built from primitives** (spheres, boxes, capsules). Rejected: "other cars are horrible", "bikes look weird from the side". The lighting and buildings were the best part.
3. **A sport bike modelled in Blender from code** to Yamaha R15 V4 measurements (1990 mm long, 1325 mm wheelbase, 815 mm seat, 17" wheels): panelled fairing, split-spoke wheels, drilled discs, chain and sprockets, rendered in Cycles. Rejected as still not realistic.
4. **A rider from MakeHuman's CC0 body**, rigged and posed in a racing crouch, with a painted suit and a modelled helmet. Rejected: "weird alien".

The lesson: realism needs **models made by 3D artists** and **real photo textures and lighting**. Code-built geometry, however careful, doesn't get there.

## Things the full-screen look must reconcile with AGENTS.md

AGENTS.md has rules every game follows. A full-screen 3D game changes how some of them are met; decide how, and tell the owner, when building:

- **The top bar and score panel:** every game has the language switch, the mute button (`#soundBtn`) and a `.stats` score you tap to pause. In full screen they become small overlays on the game.
- **Played upright on a phone** (`upright` module): it covers a phone turned sideways. Keep it, unless the owner wants landscape on phones. Ask.
- **No pause button; P / Esc, blur and leaving the page pause.**
- **Touch controls with no buttons under the board**, shown with the `gestures` component.
- **Saves, sounds, the credit line, translations** (English and Bangla, the owner reviews them) and tests, as for every game.
- **A big library** (three.js, and any loaders) is **vendored in the module** with its licence and fetched on first need. The models and textures are vendored too. Nothing loads from a CDN or any site but bdnix.com.
- **Page weight:** keep the models light enough for a phone browser. Simplify heavy meshes, compress textures, and load assets progressively.

## Built (twenty-fifth round)

The game is built on the approved look, in `apps/king-of-streets/`:

- `street-core.js`: the rules (unchanged since the twenty-fourth round). `street-scene-core.js`: the road laid out in 3D (its bends turning it, never doubling back), the chunks to build, the chase camera, the rider's view framing both hands, the engines' revs and gears and how a sound is heard (unit tested in `test/scene.test.mjs`).
- `street-3d.js`: three.js, the sky and light for each time of day, the city streamed a chunk (60 m) at a time, the racers, traffic (CNGs with uniformed drivers, standing by the parked ones), people walking, the chase camera and the rider's view. Models far off are plain (`*_lo.glb`) and merged into a few draw calls; near ones detailed.
- `street-city.js`: the buildings, shops, signboards, footpaths, kerbs, poles and cables, trees, mosques, the lake, and the bridges, flyover and expressway.
- `street-rider.js`: the riders put into the poses baked for each bike (`data/poses.json`, from the lookdev's `rider.js` grip fitting, run once by `docs/plans/street-kids-lookdev/` with the shipped models), helmets, punches, kicks and swings.
- `street-engine.js`: engine and traffic sound (Web Audio), muted with everything else.
- `street.js`: the start card, the garage (bikes, rider, race, camera, graphics), quick race, the HUD, controls, pausing and saving (`bdnix_kingofstreets_save`; the career in `bdnix_kingofstreets_career`).
- Assets: `models/` (CC BY, credited in `models/CREDITS.md`), `textures/` (CC0), published at `/assets/street/`. About 38 MB in all; a race loads only what it shows, the plain models first.
- Tests draw no frames (the test browser draws in software, seconds a frame): `test/street.mjs` turns `bdnixStreet3d.draw` off; `test/graphics.spec.mjs` draws one small frame per route.
- Not done yet: mirrors and a working dashboard in the rider's view; crashes are a lean-over fall, not a tumble.

## Owner's verdict on the first build, and the rework (twenty-sixth round)

The owner played the preview on an iPhone 15 Pro: "extremely slow, doesn't properly load, extremely dark", "no sounds" (then sound after a long load), "I don't even know how to beat up opponents… which buttons", "not smooth… pixelated graphics", "doesn't feel like a motorcycle game", and the UI tests at 32 to 45 minutes are not acceptable. They want both ways to steer on a phone, tilting and dragging, picked by the player. Nothing goes to master until it's worthwhile.

What was wrong, measured:

- The start card loaded a whole race's city behind it: 13.6 MB and a city built before it could be used; a race fetched about 30 MB.
- A frame drew 3.7 to 3.9 million triangles in about 900 draw calls: detailed riders up to 81,000 triangles and bikes up to 149,000, all racers drawn detailed, and shopfront scans (9,000 triangles each, instanced by the dozen) casting shadows.
- Phones were set to "low" graphics, drawn at one pixel per screen point: on a 3× iPhone screen, a third of its resolution.
- People on the footpaths drew as big blobs: merging a figure's parts used the first part's unpacking (the positions are stored compressed, each part unpacked by its own skeleton's inverse bind matrices) for all of them.
- The chase camera was eased along the road, so it fell further behind the faster the bike went: about 5 m extra at 115 km/h, the bike small in the distance. On a phone on its side it also saw about 112 degrees across.
- The first career race was after midnight, lit almost to black (the start card showed it too).
- The engines started after the city loaded, not on a press, and iPhones keep such sound off.
- Fighting was a tap and a flick anywhere, explained only on the start card.

What changed:

- Models: detailed riders 14,000 to 16,500 triangles, bikes 20,000 to 36,000 (`tools/mid.mjs` in the lookdev folder, simplifying each part by the same share, never past a small error); riders without the hair, eyes and teeth the helmet hides (`tools/strip.mjs`); traffic one textured model each (the `*_lo.glb`, no swap to a detailed one up close); shopfront scans, crates, trees and poles 4 to 7 times lighter, the scans casting no shadows; textures WebP at quality 72, at most 512 px (1,024 for the bikes). Models 31 MB → 13 MB; a race fetches about 13.6 MB.
- Everything a race shows loads before its countdown (no pop-in); detailed racers within 22 m of the camera, one-mesh riders beyond. A frame draws 0.7 to 1 million triangles.
- The picture is drawn at up to 2 pixels per point ("high", now the default everywhere unless the machine says it's weak), coarser a step at a time if frames come slower than 50 a second, and then without shadows (`sharpness()`). `?fps` shows the frame rate.
- The start card is a picture (`textures/card.webp`, a frame of the game); the city loads when the race starts, with a percentage.
- Levels 1 and 2 swapped: the career starts on Mirpur Road at dawn; Old Dhaka after midnight comes second, lit much brighter. The graphics spec fails any route drawn darker than 70 of 255 on average (the old night measured 38.6).
- Camera: lower and closer, eased only as the bike moves across the road, wider and shuddering with speed, leaning with the bike, no more than 96 degrees across on a wide screen; the bike leans further at speed and lifts its front pulling away hard; a hit jolts the camera and flashes the screen, a knockout plays in slow motion.
- Sound starts on the start button's press; wind rises with speed, tyres squeal in a hard lean.
- Touch: Punch, Kick and Brake buttons under the thumbs; a finger anywhere else steers (multi-touch); or tilt steering (deviceorientation, turned like a handlebar, straight ahead taken from how it's held when the race starts; `turned()` and `tilt()`), picked on the start card or in the garage. When a rival is in reach the Punch button pulses and an arrow shows their side; the first race says how to steer and to punch.
- Tests: no city for the start card, seeded races made on a page with only the rules, route drawings with graphics low (shadows drawn once, on the bridge). Under CI conditions the game's specs take about 1,080 test-seconds, down from 1,600.

## Second verdict, and the rework (twenty-seventh round)

The owner, on the iPhone again: the garage is just text ("research how racing games' UI looks"); punches and kicks look unreal, don't feel like hitting anyone and are hard to time; the game starts lagging after a while; the sounds are low grade.

- **Lag:** measured with drawing off over long races: the people on the footpaths took about two thirds of the game's work each frame. Each new one was measured vertex by vertex to stand it on the ground, cloned afresh, and its skeleton (with a bone texture never freed) animated every frame however far off. Now each look is measured once, figures (and CNG drivers) are kept in a pool and reused, only those within 30 m walk, every other frame, and their skeletons are freed with the race. A chunk of city is built a piece at a time, at most 4 ms a frame, and shown once it's done. The game's work per frame halved, and the scene holds steady over a long race.
- **Fighting:** a blow locks on to the rival in reach as it's pressed (`attack().aim`), the bike edges in, and it lands if they're within `LENIENT` (1.35) times its reach; a miss says so. The fist or foot goes at the rival's chest (IK) after a wind-up; on landing the game stops for 70 ms, a burst flashes at their chest, they reel (`flinch`: the bike wobbles, the spine and head snap away) and the camera jolts. Unit tests cover the lock-on, the edging in, the miss and the flinch.
- **Garage:** racing-game style, after looking at how they're laid out: a showroom (the bike pictured large, class D to S, a rating, bars for speed, acceleration, handling and grip, one Ride / Buy button, or how many points short), every bike as a card (locked ones grey, with their price), riders and routes as picture cards. The pictures (`textures/garage/`) are rendered from the game's own models (`tools/studio.cjs` in the lookdev folder).
- **Sound:** the engines are now recordings of a real bike (public-domain Jawa 250 and 350 exhausts from Wikimedia Commons, looped seamlessly as WAV), a low and a high loop crossfaded and pitched with the revs, each bike voiced by its pitch and brightness; a recorded bike rushing past plays as a rival passes close. The blows are layered thumps instead of beeps. Freesound, Pixabay, Sonniss, ZapSplat and archive.org were all refused by the network.
- The bikes' textures show their makers' badges (the garage pictures make it plain); the owner hasn't yet said whether real makes are acceptable.

## Environment

- **Sketchfab:** the proxy's injected credential for `api.sketchfab.com` is rejected ("Authentication credentials were not provided"), and it replaces any `Authorization` header sent to that host. What works: the token is in the environment variable **`SF_TOKEN`**, sent as `Authorization: Token $SF_TOKEN` to **`https://sketchfab.com/v3/...`** (the same API on the main host). `GET /v3/models/<uid>/download` returns links on `sketchfab-prod-media.s3.amazonaws.com`, which downloads fine. The download endpoint rate-limits (HTTP 429) after about 15 downloads in a row; wait and retry. `docs/plans/street-kids-lookdev/tools/sf.py` wraps this. `cdn.polyhaven.com` (thumbnails) is refused; it isn't needed.
- **Reachable:** `sketchfab.com`, `api.sketchfab.com`, `api.polyhaven.com`, `dl.polyhaven.org`, `kenney.nl`, `raw.githubusercontent.com`, PyPI and npm.
- **Refused before the owner's latest changes:** `github.com`, `codeload.github.com`, `api.github.com`, `quaternius.com`, `opengameart.org`, `www.cgtrader.com`, `poly.pizza`, `ambientcg.com`, `blendswap.com`, `free3d.com`, `turbosquid.com` and `static.makehumancommunity.org`.
- **Blender** installs as a Python module: `pip install bpy` (5.2.x, for Python 3.13) in a virtualenv. Rendering uses Cycles on the CPU. It's good for converting, simplifying and cleaning up downloaded models, and for exporting `.glb`.
- **Playwright:** the repo pins `@playwright/test` 1.56.1, which expects Chromium build 1194, but the container has build 1243 and can't download another. Point the old path at the installed one before running UI tests:
  ```bash
  mkdir -p /opt/pw-browsers/chromium_headless_shell-1194/chrome-linux
  ln -sf /opt/pw-browsers/chromium_headless_shell-1243/chrome-headless-shell-linux64/chrome-headless-shell /opt/pw-browsers/chromium_headless_shell-1194/chrome-linux/headless_shell
  touch /opt/pw-browsers/chromium_headless_shell-1194/INSTALLATION_COMPLETE /opt/pw-browsers/chromium_headless_shell-1194/DEPENDENCIES_VALIDATED
  ```
  For WebGL screenshots in headless Chromium, launch with `--use-angle=swiftshader --enable-unsafe-swiftshader --ignore-gpu-blocklist`.
