# Round 2 rider fitting (work in progress, nothing approved)

Scripts written on 2 November 2026 while proving one fit (Claudia on A3). They still use the scratch paths they were run from (`/tmp/claude-0/work/...`: the decompressed bikes from `../plain.mjs`, the downloaded rider, `bpy` in a virtualenv). Rewrite the paths before reusing.

- `gripray.py <bike> x+|x-`: ray-casts the bike's real grip and fits a circle per slice: radius, centre and axis, to 2 mm. A3: radius 19.8 mm, centre (+-0.355, 1.021, 0.322), axis (+-0.895, -0.01, -0.445).
- `seatmap.py <bike>`: the seat's top surface height on a grid.
- `fit.py <bike> out.glb`: poses a rider by aiming bones (not IK; the importer's bone tails are unusable): pelvis on the seat, torso lean, two-bone arms, feet on the pegs, then every finger phalanx placed on a circle round the grip axis so bone lengths are kept. Settings come from the environment: `LEAN`, `SEATZ`, `SEATY`, `KN` (knuckle height above the grip), `TAN` (palm tilt), `PHI0` (turn about the grip axis), `HS` (hand scale). It prints `METRIC` lines.
- `measure.py <glb>`: depth of the hand inside each grip, how many vertices are more than 2 mm inside, and the arc of the grip the hand touches.
- `collide.py <glb>`: rider vertices inside the bike (nearest-surface test; it is confused by thin parts, so treat its depths as a pointer, not a verdict).
- `views.mjs`, `cand.mjs`: draw a GLB with `preview/stage.js` from chosen cameras.
- `thick.py`, `handlen.py`: finger thickness and hand length of the rider.

What the first proof showed (A3 + Claudia, Renderpeople): with hand scale 1.15, knuckle height 24 mm and palm tilt 0, the hands are at most 1.1 mm inside the grip, nothing is more than 2 mm inside, and the hands touch 315 degrees of the grip, checked from the top, front, underneath and along the axis.

## Update, later on 2 November 2026: the woman (B2) on A3 is fitted and measured

Rider: Sophia (Renderpeople, `dc448c3be0e74f96a55fb475a13433cf`, CC BY). Claudia and Carla were dropped: both wear stiletto heels, which cannot go on a peg and are not everyday riding clothes. Helmet: Motor Helmet (Tom Verbeeck, CC BY), cut from 100,840 to 6,276 triangles (its 64,000-triangle decorative pattern part is dropped). Run with `HAIRISLAND=17 HDZ=-0.06 SEATY=0.98 RIDER=sophia_edit RSCALE=0.0104 SEATZ=-0.24 LEAN=38 HEADUP=26 PHI0=0 TAN=0 KN=0.020 HS=1.1 python fit.py A3 out.glb`, then `checkall.sh out.glb`.

What was found and done:
- Sophia's skeleton is the Renderpeople one with a name prefix; `fit.py` strips it. Her bind pose is posed (hips swayed, one shoulder higher), so `fit.py` squares the pelvis and the shoulder line first.
- Her hair is one UV island (17, 1,070 faces), removed. Hair is also painted onto the blouse texture (island 6, 44% of it): `fixtex.py` repaints those pixels as blouse fabric at 2048 px and writes a derived copy in `sophia_edit/`; the source texture is untouched. The blouse is flat white where the hair was, and its upper back has a torn-looking edge between the helmet and the shoulders, visible close up.
- Fingertips: the last finger bone's direction is taken from the mesh (her relaxed fingers are curled at rest), not from the bone before it.
- Feet: each sole is placed on its peg by measuring the sole of that foot and moving the foot until the measured sole point lands on the peg top, because the two feet differ at rest.
- Helmet: scaled to 27 cm tall, visor at eye height, pinned to the head bone; every rider face that is weighted to the head or lies inside the helmet is deleted.

Measured on the exported mesh (not yet on a compressed file): hands at most 1.4 mm inside the grips, none more than 2 mm inside, and in the central 4 cm of each hand none of the rays through the top-and-front 120 degrees of the grip reach bare grip (so the grip does not show between the fingers); seat contact at most 1.2 mm deep; soles 1.2 and 3.4 mm from the peg tops with nothing more than 2 mm inside. Rider 9,861 triangles plus helmet 6,276 = 16,137 (budget 20,000), two materials.

Still not done for round 2: the man; the other four bikes (each needs its own grips, seat and pegs measured: `gripray.py`, `seatmap.py`, and peg boxes by hand); the stand check; the lean is 38 degrees, which is a lot for a commuter bike and is set by the short arms and the seat-to-grip distance on A3; compressing to meshopt and WebP and re-measuring on the final files; recolouring the helmet per rider; the review page.


## Final state of round 2 (2 November 2026)

All ten riders on bikes (the man is Eric, the woman Sophia) were built by `batch.sh <eric|sophia> <A1..A5>`, with settings searched by `opt.sh` against `score.py`, regenerated by `finalrun2.sh` (FSQ=0.85 draws the fingers 15% together so no gap shows between them), compressed and measured on the compressed file by `verify.sh` (decompress with `plainone.mjs`, put the rider back on the bike with `merge.py`, run `checkall.sh`, `inside.py`, `gaps2.py`). `bikes.json` holds each bike's measured grip (centre, axis, radius from `gripray.py`/`gripfind.py`/`gripref.py`), seat and peg. `stand.py` poses the standing riders (B1, B2); `helmonly.mjs` takes the helmet out of a rider file (B3); `mkimgs.py` and `views3.mjs` draw the review pictures from the compressed bike and rider files; `fixtex.py` repaints the hair off the woman's top; `hue.py` recolours the helmet.
Paths are the scratch paths they were run from (`/tmp/claude-0/work`); set them to your own before running. Known weaknesses: the pegclear and seatclear checks use a straight-down ray and mislead under overhangs (read `inside.py` and the pictures first); the woman's top has a torn-looking upper edge; the scooter pose is hunched.

## Second try (3 November 2026)

`fit3.py` replaces `fit.py`: Biped skeletons, helmet auto-fit (`HFIT`), hand re-skinning (`RESKIN`), a seat solver that lowers the rider until the buttocks rest on the seat (`SEATT`), and centring. `batch3.sh <bike>` fits the woman with the settings in `cfg/best_<bike>.cfg` (found by `opt3.sh`, scored by `score.py`); `final3b.sh` and `reship.sh` compress with `optr2.mjs` (no prune, dedup or weld, which strip the helmet's graphics), put the file back on the frozen bike and measure it (`measure.py`, `gaps2.py`, `seatgap.py`, `inside.py`), then draw the review pictures (`mkimgs3.py`, `views3.mjs`). `stand3.py` makes the standing figure. `r10clean.py` paints out the helmet's logos and tints the visor; `boots.py` recolours the shoes; `helmexport.py` exports the helmet alone.

## Third try (3 November 2026)

`fit4.py` is `fit3.py` plus the weight-on-seat step (`SQ1`, `SQ2`); `fit6.py` and `stand6.py` add `MALE=1` (the reshape). `batch4.sh`/`final4.sh`/`reship4.sh` run the woman; `batchm.sh`/`finalm.sh`/`reshipm.sh` the man (`RIDER=racerman`, the suit recoloured blue). `air.py` measures how much of the underside lies on the seat; `cut.mjs` draws the cut-away; `collect2.py` gathers every number into `reviews/round2b/metrics.json`. `retex_eric.py` is the businessman recolour that was tried and dropped.
